STAGE 1. OVER 100 YEARS OF COOPERATION From Historical Connections to Academic Partnership

Let us begin with the history of cooperation between two universities: Dnipro University of Technology in Ukraine and TU Bergakademie Freiberg in Germany. Although the official institutional partnership developed over time, its roots go back to the second half of the nineteenth century. One of the key figures in this story was Oleksandr Pol, a prominent entrepreneur and public figure who discovered significant iron ore deposits near Kryvyi Rih in 1866. Recognising the importance of these discoveries, he turned to specialists at the Freiberg Mining Academy for scientific assistance. Professor Carl Bernhard von Cotta conducted chemical and mineralogical analyses of the ore samples. Following these investigations, the German engineer Leo Strippelmann was invited to explore the deposits in the Kryvyi Rih region between 1872 and 1874. His research confirmed the richness of these iron ore resources.

These discoveries became an important foundation for the industrial development of the region. The railway connection established in 1884 between the iron ore deposits of Kryvyi Rih and the coal resources of Donbas further contributed to the development of metallurgy. Another important milestone came in 1899, when the Katerynoslav Higher Mining School was established. It became the first higher education institution in the region and the direct predecessor of today's Dnipro University of Technology. The academic connection with Freiberg continued through the exchange of knowledge, professional training and scientific contacts. Many engineers and future university lecturers had undertaken internships or further studies abroad, including at Bergakademie Freiberg.

This historical background demonstrates that cooperation between our universities was not created by a formal agreement alone. It developed from shared scientific interests and a common understanding of the importance of mining education and technological progress.
