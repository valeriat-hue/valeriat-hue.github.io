STAGE 4. THE INFLUENCE OF FREIBERG ON THE FORMATION OF SCIENTIFIC SCHOOLS AT DNIPRO UNIVERSITY OF TECHNOLOGY From Scientific Expertise to Lasting Academic Traditions

While the previous stage focused primarily on Joseph Tanatar, this part of the exhibition broadens our perspective and demonstrates that the influence of Freiberg extended to several generations of Ukrainian scientists and engineers. The development of scientific education in Katerynoslav was closely connected with the transfer of European knowledge, technologies and professional experience. One of the earliest examples was the collaboration involving Carl Bernhard von Cotta, Oleksandr Pol and Leo Strippelmann. Their geological investigations of the Kryvyi Rih iron ore basin helped establish the scientific basis for the region's industrial development and contributed to the emergence of specialised mining education. Another important figure was Pavlo Rubin, a professor of metallurgy who visited Bergakademie Freiberg and the Düsseldorf Industrial Exhibition in 1902. His professional experience contributed to the transfer of advanced German metallurgical practices into Ukrainian engineering education. In 1905, he published research on fuel and combustion technologies.

We should also mention Volodymyr Huskov, who became the founder of the Department of Mineral Processing. In 1903, the Katerynoslav Higher Mining School received nine models of jigging machines and other mineral-processing equipment from Freiberg. These teaching models became an important part of laboratory training for future mining engineers. This is an early example of educational technology transfer between the two institutions. Another important personality was Heorhii Yevreinov, a graduate of the Katerynoslav Higher Mining School in 1903 and the founder of the Department of Mining Electrical Engineering. In 1908, he undertook a scientific mission to Germany, maintaining and developing connections with European scientific and technical centres.

Together, these stories demonstrate that the influence of Freiberg was not limited to one scientist or one academic discipline. It contributed to the development of geology, geochemistry, metallurgy, mineral processing and mining electromechanics. The exhibition therefore presents the history of cooperation through individual people, their professional achievements and the knowledge they transferred between academic communities. The key message is that international academic cooperation can shape entire scientific schools, influence educational institutions and contribute to the technological development of a country.
